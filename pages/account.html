An account, without the usual parts

No email address, no real name, nothing about you. The site picks a username for you — two ordinary words — and you choose a password. That is the whole account.

With one, you can:

  • Save cameras. A star appears next to every camera in the list on the map page. What you save is visible to you and to nobody else.
  • Report a camera. Seen one that is not on the map, or one that no longer works? A new tab appears at the top for sending it in, and reports earn points on the leaderboard.

    On the leaderboard

    The leaderboard is public: anyone can read it, signed in or not. Your username says nothing about you, but what someone has reported, and how much, is a pattern. You can keep yours off the list; the points still count.

    Change your password

    The current one first, so a session left open on another machine cannot set a new one. There is no email on the account: this is the only way it changes.

    At least 10 characters, with a capital, a number and a symbol.

    Sign out everywhere

    Every device that is signed in as this account is signed out, this one included. For a session left open on a shared or borrowed machine.

    This ends every session for , here and everywhere else, and signing back in needs the password. A device that is signed in now keeps going until its current token runs out — up to an hour — and cannot renew it after that.

    Delete this account

    What goes: the account and its password, your XP, your saved cameras, and every report you sent, with any photo attached to it.

    What stays: a camera that is on the map because of a report of yours stays on the map. It is part of the record now, and deleting an account does not un-see a camera. It keeps the date it was approved and nothing about you.

    The username is released. The same two words may one day be drawn for someone else, and nothing will connect them to this account. There is no undo: a deleted account cannot be recovered, because nothing about it is kept.
